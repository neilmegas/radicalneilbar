The Labour Group on Sheffield City Council this week announced that they would not be withdrawing from their power-sharing arrangement with the Green Group. The Labour leader Fran Belbin wrote to Green group leader Angela Argenzio on the 16th October, stating demands which councillor Argenzio rejected . The Labour Party declared on 2nd November that Labour would end the power-sharing arrangement on Sheffield City Council. At a full Sheffield City Council meeting on 4th November, Councillor Belbin announced a U-turn from her and her national party’s previous declarations, explaining that the two parties “would work more constructively together going forward”. On Thursday, DEFRA Secretary Angela Eagle stated that “what happens in Sheffield will be a matter for both the party chair and the NEC to decide,” confirming that the NEC’s position is unchanged.. A spokesperson for the Green Party said: “In Sheffield, the Green Group have stood firmly by their principles, and are continuing to put residents above political point-scoring. The local Labour Party have also realised that smashing apart their administration at the whims of Labour HQ isn’t in residents’ interests. “Andy Burnham’s authoritarian diktat has angered councillors and residents across the country, including those in his own party. The Labour Party leadership’s attempts to force local councillors to tear up agreements they’ve worked hard to maintain have resulted in their own councillors defying this overreach. “The Labour Party leadership’s support for Israel seems to have no limit – arming a genocide, locking up pensioners for holding up pieces of paper, and now attempting to disrupt successful local partnerships from delivering for residents.
