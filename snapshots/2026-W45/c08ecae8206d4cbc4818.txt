The Green Party, and the Green Group Office, representing Green councillors across the country, have been hugely disappointed in moves designed by national parties to force local council leaders into jeopardising successful progressive partnerships that are delivering benefits for residents. Across the country, Green, Labour, Independent and Liberal Democrat councillors have worked together to provide stable local government, protect services and deliver positive change. Leaders across these parties are rightly proud of what those partnerships have achieved. Those who issued ultimatums that destabilised power sharing agreements have often ignored the judgements of their own local leaders, failed to respect the choices made by local voters, and are disrupting successful partnerships. Partnership is at the heart of Green politics. We believe in working together for the good of people and planet, even when that means working alongside people and parties with whom we disagree on some national and international issues. With the serious cost of living issues currently impacting so many people across our country, it is even more important we work together to deliver solutions. We are a democratic party that trusts local leaders to make the right decisions for the people they represent. The range of responses to the ultimatums reflects the diversity and anti racist nature of our Party and of local circumstances and partnerships across the country. It is right that this democracy is constantly evolving; as our membership and representation expand, our party democracy must be updated – creating wider engagement for members, giving leaders more responsibility to set the party’s strategic direction, and making our processes as robust and accessible as possible. That work has been in train for many months, and continues. The Green Party believes top-down directives to break up and disrupt locally-run services are not in the interests of the residents we represent. We trust local communities, local councillors and local leaders to know what is best for them. We ask that others do the same.
