Rachel Millward letter to Angela Eagle: “I am concerned that you are putting the advice of water industry shareholders and their well-paid lobbyists above the interests of the British people. “This bureaucratic proposal only adds to the sense of a tinkering government that talks big but delivers very little. As the Chancellor has said, this is a continuity government.” Full letter to Environment Secretary Rt Hon Dame Angela Eagle Secretary of State for Environment, Food and Rural Affairs Department for Environment, Food and Rural Affairs 5th November 2026 Dear Dame Angela, I am writing to raise serious concerns about the announcement of eight regional water councils, and to demand answers about how they will address the crisis in our water industry. The Prime Minister has repeatedly talked about public ownership or control of water. What we have been offered instead are councils that appear to be advisory only, with very little real power. More public oversight is welcome in principle, but it is clearly not enough to bring about the change we need. How, in practice, will these councils hold private water companies to account? Will companies have any legal duty to act on their recommendations? What powers will the councils have when companies fail to meet their responsibilities? This bureaucratic proposal only adds to the sense of a tinkering government that talks big but delivers very little. As the Chancellor has said, this is a continuity government. The scale of the crisis demands far more than another layer of bureaucracy. Thames Water and South East Water have categorically failed in their duties. Here in Sussex and across the South East, residents have become far too used to driving to collect bottled water when there is nothing in our taps. No major reservoir has been built since privatisation began, despite planning permissions being granted. Much of the nation has endured hosepipe bans all summer, whilst a trillion litres of water are leaked every year. Our rivers are in crisis too. Not a single river in the UK has a good ecological rating. Illegal raw sewage spills destroy plant and animal life and present a serious danger to public health. For all this, in England we pay some of the highest water bills in Europe, whilst shareholders have taken over £85 billion in dividends since privatisation, and creditors have taken more still in interest. The public is paying the price for a system that has rewarded investors while allowing essential infrastructure to deteriorate. We do not need toothless water councils. We need failing water companies to be taken into public ownership without further delay. Water companies have systematically breached their primary statutory duties to keep sewage treatment and pipes in good repair, and shareholders and creditors have profited from that pollution. It is time to take these failing companies into public ownership, without compensation. Most Thames Water shareholders have already written off their investments and declared the company “uninvestable”. The market has already priced the debt as junk, and repair costs exceed it: repairing Thames Water’s broken sewage and pipe networks will cost over £23 billion to 2029 alone, more than its £20 billion of debt. Water industry lobbyists may declare public ownership too complicated and too expensive. But there is a way forward. So I ask, on behalf of the ripped-off public: what is the government waiting for? I am concerned that you are putting the advice of water industry shareholders and their well-paid lobbyists above the interests of the British people? I therefore also request full disclosure of all formal and informal contacts that your department, the Cabinet Office and the Prime Minister’s Office have had with representatives of the water industry, including the Prime Minister’s Chief of Staff, who was formerly a water industry lobbyist. The public deserves to know whether the government is acting in their interests, or those of the industry that has brought our water system to its knees.
