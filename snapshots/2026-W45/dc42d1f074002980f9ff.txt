Ahead of COP31 beginning next week, leader of the Parliamentary Green Party Dr Ellie Chowns MP has written to the Prime Minister urging him to publish a roadmap to phase out all tax breaks for fossil fuel companies – a step which the UK has already committed to but is yet to deliver. Alongside publishing a tax break phase out plan, Dr Chowns has called on the PM to take further steps to align the UK tax system with the reality of our climate commitments, including: – Strengthening the successor to the Energy Profits Levy, the Oil and Gas Revenue Levy, which is projected to be a significant tax cut for industry to the tune of £8.6 billion ( Global Witness, 2026 ). – Pushing for a global profit tax on international oil companies through the UN Tax Convention, channelling revenues via UN climate funds to support communities being hit by climate impacts. Dr Chowns has also issued the following comment ahead of COP31: “Less than ten years ago the UK took pride in being a climate leader on the global stage, with overwhelming cross-party consensus on the need for urgent action to reduce emissions and transition away from climate-wrecking fossil fuels. How quickly we have fallen. “Against the political backdrop of the Tories abandoning their commitment to net zero and Reform UK adopting outright climate-denialism, it is unforgiveable that Andy Burnham’s Labour is also now taking shocking steps backward when it comes to our climate commitments: approving Jackdaw, refusing to rule out greenlighting Rosebank oilfield, and cutting funding for vital home insultation and clean heating programmes.“ Greens are clear that urgently tackling the climate crisis is critical if we are to protect Britain’s families, public services, and economy from the devastation of climate breakdown – and we will keep up pressure on Andy Burnham to give this existential threat the attention and action it demands at COP31 next week. ” Notes to editors: The full text of the letter reads: Dear Prime Minister, I am writing to ask you to publish a roadmap to phase out all tax breaks for fossil fuel companies at COP31 in Antalya next week, a step which the UK has already committed to but is yet to deliver. £13.3 billion of UK public money has been spent on tax breaks for fossil fuel firms between 2019 and 2026 ( DESNZ, 2026 ). This is despite the huge profits North Sea oil and gas companies have reported ( Greenpeace, 2026 ). Instead of funding climate breakdown, this money could have fully funded seven years of support for North Sea workers to securely transition to renewable industries – or insulated every fuel-poor home in England, a total of 3.17 million homes, significantly bringing down energy bills ( Greenpeace, 2026 ). The publication of the UK’s fossil fuel incentives and subsidies inventory was a welcome first step that has provided greater transparency. But it has also highlighted the extent of UK fiscal incentives to the fossil fuel industry ( DESNZ, 2026 ). In order to meet our climate commitments and put the brakes on catastrophic global warming and worsening extreme weather here in the UK and worldwide, the UK must now redirect these incentives away from fossil fuels and towards renewable energy. This would enable us to raise critical finance for climate action, including protecting communities from the impacts of the climate emergency, while bring down emissions, sending a clear signal to investors and businesses about the speed and urgency of the energy transition. After the hottest summer on record, you committed to attending COP31 in recognition of the urgency of the climate crisis. It would be a missed opportunity and a blow to the UK’s credibility if you do not use this platform to make meaningful climate commitments on the world stage. This must include publishing a tax break phase out plan, and re-committing to ending all new drilling, including the Rosebank oilfield. Alongside publishing a tax break phase out plan, there are further steps the UK must take to align the tax system with the reality of our climate commitments, including: – Strengthening the successor to the Energy Profits Levy, the Oil and Gas Revenue Levy, which is projected to be a significant tax cut for industry to the tune of £8.6 billion ( Global Witness, 2026 ). – Pushing for a global profit tax on international oil companies through the UN Tax Convention, channelling revenues via UN climate funds to support communities being hit by climate impacts. The UK’s latest round of International Climate Finance, ICF4, will provide around £6 billion over the course of 2026-29. This represents a real terms drop of over 50% from the previous round, once inflation and various accounting practices are incorporated ( Carbon Brief, 2026 ). Cutting back on vital support will only further undermine trust in international climate talks and critically derail the process. It is vital that the UK increases provision of public finance for climate action. Taxing polluters is one way that the government can raise additional funds for that purpose. COP 31 is your opportunity to change course on the climate – to choose transformative action that tackles injustice, inequality and the affordability crisis alongside meeting our climate commitments. Ending tax breaks to fossil fuel companies is a vital next step on that path and one that must be taken in Antalya next week.
