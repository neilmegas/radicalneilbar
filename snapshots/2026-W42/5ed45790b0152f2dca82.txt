Green Party Leader Zack Polanski has been reacting to the result of the Holborn and St Pancras by-election, which saw the party’s second strongest result in a Parliamentary by-election ever, more than tripling its vote share achieved in 2024. The by-election was triggered just over a month ago, when the constituency’s former representative – and Labour’s former prime minister – Keir Starmer stood down as MP. Considered one of Labour’s safest seats, it has been held by the party for decades. “What will really stick with me… is all the amazing people” Zack congratulated Labour’s Sagal Abdi-Wali on being elected MP, “wish[ing] her the very best” in representing the “amazing place” of Holborn and St Pancras. In a video posted this morning, Zack reflected on an “amazing few weeks” on the campaign. “What will really stick with me from this campaign is all the amazing people I’ve met.” “From the women at Crossroads Community Centre to the volunteers who have been out there giving so much of their free time to door knocking and leafleting.” “It’s genuinely been inspiring how many people came round in this campaign.” Thank you Holborn & St Pancras.Our second best byelection result ever. — Zack Polanski (@zackpolanski.bsky.social) 2026-10-09T05:19:55.819Z Second best by-election result Despite his disappointment, Zack also reflected on what was a strong result for the Green Party. The 33% Zack received was more than three times the vote share the Green Party received in 2024, and a 22 percentage point increase. Speaking to BBC Breakfast this morning, Zack acknowledged his disappointment – “No politician runs an election and doesn’t want to win” – but stressed how “incredibly proud” he was of both the result and the campaign. “I knew we had a good chance of taking a run at the seat, and in fact what we got was the second best ever by-election result in Green Party history.” Second, of course, only to Hannah Spencer’s stunning victory over Labour and Reform in Gorton and Denton earlier this year. The Green Party’s ability to challenge Labour in such seats has not gone unnoticed. As Zack told BBC Breakfast, independent pollster John Curtice acknowledged this morning that “the Green Party are more of an electoral threat in these seats than we’ve ever been before”. And the Green Party’s challenge to Labour won’t end here. “People voted Green and then we moved Labour move” It is easy forget in a year of frantic political change, but the Green Party’s impact on the direction of the country has been seismic. At the start of the year, the strategy of Labour PM Keir Starmer was seemingly to ‘out-Reform’ Reform. But since then the Green Party has given Labour one headache after another. In February, Hannah Spencer’s victory over Labour and Reform in Gorton and Denton signalled the start of the end for Keir Starmer. The local elections in May were the final straw. The Green Party won big in former Labour heartlands, and took four times as many votes off Labour as did Reform. In short, the Green Party pushed Keir Starmer out of No. 10.
