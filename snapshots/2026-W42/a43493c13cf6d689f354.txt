Sure enough, SLF deposited $83 million into Texas PAC’s coffers the following day.” It would be wild if Paxton still loses. Paxton has been embroiled in rolling scandals for years, and some current polls have him underwater by 13 points. A New York Times /Siena survey found only 38 percent of likely Texas voters believe he has “good character,” versus 56 percent for Talarico. Paxton was indicted on securities fraud charges in 2015, and that case dragged on for most of a decade before ending in a pretrial agreement. Then the Texas House, led by Republicans, accused him of bribery and abuse of public trust and successfully impeached him in 2023. The charges centered on allegations that he used his office to help donor Nate Paul—so you can imagine why Musk might want to curry favor with Paxton. Though Paxton was later acquitted by the state Senate, scandal keeps chasing him: Last year, his wife, state Sen. Angela Paxton, filed for divorce on “biblical” grounds, citing adultery as one of the reasons. Of course, many Republican voters at the state level saw the impeachment as nothing more than a political hit job, one echoing what’s happened to President Donald Trump at the national level. But what far-right, already-convinced Republicans believe about Paxton is sort of beside the point: He needs turnout, and he needs to win over more moderate Republicans and maybe even some fence-sitters if he hopes to beat Talarico. It’s possible Musk’s cash infusion will help, but it’s also possible a cash infusion of many millions of dollars won’t be enough to rehabilitate Paxton’s eroded image. A poll from the University of Texas and Texas Politics Project conducted in August “contained four major warning signs for Paxton: a drop in his support from June, lagging GOP motivation to vote, worse favorability numbers than Talarico and Trump’s approval being underwater in the state.” Scenes from New York: When Gov. Kathy Hochul first ran for re-election, Republicans in New York tried to prey on fears about crime, rising inflation and illegal immigration to boost the chances of their statewide ticket in the crucial 2022 midterms, reports The New York Times . The strategy seemed to work. Republicans flipped four House seats in New York, while Hochul herself barely held on with a six-point victory over Lee Zeldin, who at the time represented Long Island and now leads the EPA. This year, Republicans have tapped Bruce Blakeman, the Nassau County executive, as the candidate with the best odds to topple Hochul, again campaigning on crime fears. Yet the approach isn’t delivering the same punch as before. To my mind, crime and public disorder remain concerns, but they aren’t as pervasive as they were at the height of the pandemic era. And they aren’t viewed as disastrously as they once were.
