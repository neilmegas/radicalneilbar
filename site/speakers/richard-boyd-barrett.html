People Before Profit Support Garvaghy Road Residents in Portadown

People Before Profit members mobilised to Portadown in solidarity with Garvaghy Road residents opposing an Orange Order march as a High Court appeal was heard in Belfast, with party representatives condemning political unionism for stoking sectarian division.

Source metadataAI-assisted screening and summaryNot human-validated
Political unionism is in deep crisis, wracked by scandal and unable to offer anything to workers. In this crisis, they have chosen to stoke the fires of sectarian division by pushing the plan for this march forward. We will not let them drag us back to the dark days of sectarian violence and division. I will continue to support the residents and stand against any attempt to whip up hatred again.
Gerry Carroll
Last week in Leinster House, myself and Richard Boyd Barrett met with residents on Garvaghy Road. We heard heartbreaking stories from the 1990s - a horrific, dark period where 21 members of the community were the victims of sectarian killings as the Orange Order fought to force an unwanted march down the Garvaghy Road. We made a commitment to stand with them and we will maintain that commitment as long as we have to.
Paul Murphy

TDs Vote Today on People Before Profit Motion for €500 Electricity Credits Funded by 20 Cent Levy on Data Centres

People Before Profit TDs brought a motion to the Dáil on 23 September 2026 calling for a 20 cent per kilowatt-hour levy on data centre electricity consumption to fund €500 household electricity credits, and for a ban on new data centres.

Source metadataAI-assisted screening and summaryNot human-validated
Households in Ireland pay the highest electricity prices in the EU. At about €0.40 per kilowatt hour, that's 38% higher than the EU average. However, data centres, which consume a quarter of our electricity, pay an average of about €0.20 per kWh, half the price paid by households.
Richard Boyd Barrett
This €0.20 levy would raise €1.5 billion , more than enough to pay for a €500 annual electricity credit for every household and an additional €500 electricity credit for households with people with disabilities.
Richard Boyd Barrett

Budget Office: 95% of Households Are Better Off Under People Before Profit’s Proposals Than Any Others

People Before Profit released Budget 2026 proposals that the Parliamentary Budget Office analyzed as benefiting 95% of households with average income gains of 9.4%, funded by €46.4 billion in net revenue raising primarily through corporation and capital taxes.

Source metadataAI-assisted screening and summaryNot human-validated
The report from the Parliamentary Budget Office demonstrates just how progressive People Before Profit's 2026 budget proposals are. Under our proposals, 95% of households would be better off, while the wealthiest 5% and corporations would pay significantly higher taxes to begin the redistribution of income and wealth that is so urgently required.
Richard Boyd Barrett
By taxing multi-millionaires and big corporations, we demonstrated that we can increase average incomes for middle income households by 14.2%. According to the PBO this compares to 1.4% increase from the Social Democrats' proposals for middle income earners, 3.2% by Labour and 4.5% by Sinn Féin.
Richard Boyd Barrett

People Before Profit-Solidarity Think-In: €500 electricity credit funded by data centres levy

People Before Profit-Solidarity held a Think-In on 10 September 2026 proposing a €500 electricity credit funded by a 20c per kWh levy on data centres, wealth taxes on multi-millionaires, price caps on energy and groceries, and announced plans to use Private Members Business time in the Dáil to demand data centres pay household electricity rates.

Source metadataAI-assisted screening and summaryNot human-validated
At our Think-In we will discuss the cost-of-living-crisis and our proposals for meaningful measures to address it. One of our key proposals is for €500 electricity credits for households to be funded by an electricity consumption levy of 20c per kwh on data centres. This would make a big difference to households facing crippling energy bills this winter.
Paul Murphy
We will use our Private Members Business time in the Dáil next week to demand that data centres pay the same for their electricity as householders and that the funds raised be used to fund €500 electricity credits for households.
Paul Murphy
