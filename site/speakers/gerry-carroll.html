People Before Profit representatives including MLA Gerry Carroll, TD Paul Murphy, and Dublin City Councillor Conor Reddy joined residents in an overnight blockade of Garvaghy Road to prevent an Orange Order march, with over a thousand people participating in solidarity.

People Before Profit Support Garvaghy Road Residents in Portadown

People Before Profit members mobilised to Portadown in solidarity with Garvaghy Road residents opposing an Orange Order march as a High Court appeal was heard in Belfast, with party representatives condemning political unionism for stoking sectarian division.

Source metadataAI-assisted screening and summaryNot human-validated
Political unionism is in deep crisis, wracked by scandal and unable to offer anything to workers. In this crisis, they have chosen to stoke the fires of sectarian division by pushing the plan for this march forward. We will not let them drag us back to the dark days of sectarian violence and division. I will continue to support the residents and stand against any attempt to whip up hatred again.
Gerry Carroll
Last week in Leinster House, myself and Richard Boyd Barrett met with residents on Garvaghy Road. We heard heartbreaking stories from the 1990s - a horrific, dark period where 21 members of the community were the victims of sectarian killings as the Orange Order fought to force an unwanted march down the Garvaghy Road. We made a commitment to stand with them and we will maintain that commitment as long as we have to.
Paul Murphy

People Before Profit politicians Gerry Carroll and Richard Boyd Barrett condemned the Parades Commission's decision to permit an Orange Order march along Garvaghy Road, calling it a capitulation to unionist pressure and urging both British and Irish governments to prevent the march.

Source metadataAI-assisted screening and summaryNot human-validated
This is a shameful decision. 28 years of peace on the Garvaghy Road was not a problem in need of fixing. Residents made their opposition to this march known in overwhelming numbers, and they have been ignored.
Gerry Carroll
Since the High Court ruling, the Orange Order and unionist politicians have waged a campaign of pressure against the Parades Commission. Days later, the Commission has delivered them what they always wanted. This sequence of events will not be lost on anyone.
Gerry Carroll

Carroll Brings Toxic Lough Neagh Water into Assembly Chamber: "Would any MLA Take a Sip?"

People Before Profit MLA Gerry Carroll brought a jar of toxic algae-contaminated water from Lough Neagh into the Northern Ireland Assembly chamber to highlight the water crisis, challenged MLAs to drink it, blamed the Executive's Going for Growth strategy and factory farms for the pollution, accused the DUP of using Drumcree sectarian tensions as distraction, and announced party participation in a March for Clean Water at Stormont on 26 September 2026.

Source metadataAI-assisted screening and summaryNot human-validated
What I held up in the chamber today is Lough Neagh. It's green, it stinks, and it's toxic. And it is the source of at least 40% of our drinking water. People are expected to drink this, to bathe their children in it and to swim in it. I asked MLAs today whether any of them would take a sip. Not one of them would, and yet they expect the public to live with it.
Gerry Carroll
This disaster was created by the Going for Growth strategy the Executive parties and official opposition signed up to, by factory farms waved through on the lough shore, by underinvestment in sewage infrastructure and by a total failure to crack down on agri-polluters.
Gerry Carroll
