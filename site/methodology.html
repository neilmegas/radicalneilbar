Methodology and revision log

RAPPORT is an evidence-led, automated AI monitoring archive powered by the Claude API. It records what the listed parties did and published; absence from the archive must not be interpreted as political inactivity.

How one weekly report is made

1
Collect

Check configured party sites and feeds, official parliamentary records, and configured discovery searches.

2
Preserve

Store the source URL and a local text snapshot so later deletion or editing does not erase the record.

3
Screen

Retain dated, substantive material about a monitored party; keep rejected rows visible in the review audit.

4
Describe

Extract a factual summary, named actors, verbatim quotations, and an observable action type.

5
Present

Give direct party documents full evidence cards; present outside reporting as linked context.

6
Compare

Compare retained action types and direct-document volume with the preceding archived week.

Unit of analysis

Evidence record: One dated, source-addressable party document, parliamentary intervention, leader statement, or external report describing a substantive observable act by or about one monitored party. A record is not a claim, sentence, social-media post fragment, party-week, or ideological theme.

Duplicate rule: Substantively identical syndicated reports are clustered; the evidence record remains attached to the stable source URL used at collection.

Time rule: Records are assigned to the ISO week containing their recorded publication date. Undated pages are not treated as dated evidence.

The unit is not a party-week, ideological topic, sentence, or estimate of public opinion.

What the labels mean

Direct record means a party-controlled source, a named leader’s direct channel, or an official parliamentary record. Outside reporting means journalism or another contextual source. Action labels describe the form of an observable act, such as a parliamentary intervention or mobilisation; they are not ideological topic labels. Far-left and far-right are the two operational roster buckets used throughout this project. They are not claims of academic consensus or party self-identification.

Coverage and limits

Collection coverage is reported party by party. “No dated activity retained” means the configured source was checked but produced no qualifying dated record. “Official source inaccessible” means the check failed. “No archived check” means this database contains no collection log for that party and week. Search fallback use is shown separately. Automated collection can still miss posts, dynamic pages, deleted content, and material on unconfigured platforms.

Translations and model-written interpretations can contain errors. Original text, source links, prompt-version records, confidence labels, and the audit trail exist so a reader can verify the evidence. Week-over-week change describes the retained archive, not the totality of a party’s behaviour.

Negative-evidence protocol

A missing item is not a negative finding. The following rules govern any statement about silence or non-observation:

  1. A blank party-week never means that the party was inactive.
  2. "No dated activity retained" is used only when at least one configured collection channel completed successfully.
  3. "Official source inaccessible" and "No archived check" are reported separately and must not be interpreted as silence.
  4. Search fallback is disclosed because search indexes are selective and change over time.
  5. Comparisons use retained records and coverage denominators; raw document counts alone are not behavioural measures.

AI-output labelling

Every full evidence card distinguishes source metadata and source quotations from AI-assisted screening, summary, translation, translation audit, and interpretation. A separate human-coded label appears only where a researcher code is stored. No unlabelled model output should be read as source evidence.

See the versioned prompt archive, quality and validation dashboard, and party-inclusion dossiers.
